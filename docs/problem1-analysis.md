# Problem 1 — analysis for the report

Figure 6 shows severe attenuation of hidden-state gradients toward early time steps at T=100. After training, the ratio of the mean norm at t=1 to that at t=100 is about 3.6×10⁻²² for RNN, 1.2×10⁻¹¹ for LSTM and 6.5×10⁻⁵ for GRU. The gated models retain a stronger early hidden-state signal in these probes, especially GRU. Predictions and recurrent parameter gradients match the library models, checking that the unrolled computation graph is correct. These norms also depend on the loss and state trajectory. LSTM’s separate cell-state path is not fully represented by dL/dh_t, so the plot cannot establish a complete memory ranking.

Table 2 and Figures 4–5 show that none of the models solved the adding task at any tested length under five epochs: all 36 runs exceed MSE 0.05 and remain near their empirical constant-1 baselines. Training at T=200 plateaus around 0.17. The lower MSE at T=400 also occurs for constant prediction and is not evidence of improved memory. The short budget prevents the anticipated contrast between successful gated models and a failing RNN; stronger gradient signals alone did not produce successful marked-value addition here.

The odd-ID experiment isolates initial forget bias while pairing other weights and shuffle sequences. Table 4/Figure 8 give test MSE 0.16840±0.00045 for bias 0 and 0.16849±0.00039 for bias 1, with zero solved runs for either. This tiny difference relative to seed variation does not support a reliable benefit or harm from bias 1 under this budget. It does not rule out a benefit after longer training.

On HAR, Table 1 shows RNN accuracy 70.52±4.91%, LSTM 87.71±0.29% and GRU 88.19±1.04%. Figure 2 highlights sitting/standing confusion in the validation-selected GRU. Gated cells improve this classification task despite the unsuccessful adding experiment. Table 3/Figure 7 show diminishing accuracy gains with width: GRU grows from 87.41±0.73% at h=32 to 89.04±0.70% at h=128, while parameters grow from 4,326 to 54,150. LSTM h=128 reaches 88.28±0.43% with 71,942 parameters. CPU training times favor LSTM over comparable GRU widths on this backend; they do not measure deployment inference energy.

For a memory-constrained wearable, I would prototype GRU h=32. It uses about 71% fewer parameters than GRU h=64 for a 0.78 percentage-point lower mean accuracy, and beats LSTM h=32 in this sweep. Its float32 weights occupy about 16.9 KiB, excluding activations and runtime overhead. This recommendation follows the measured accuracy/size trade-off; battery life and latency still require device measurements. Three seeds, provisional variant settings and short training budgets limit generalization and statistical certainty.
